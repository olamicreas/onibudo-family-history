Family Origins

Olawolu “Onibudo” was a renowned Ibadan warrior (Jagun-jagun Ibadan) descended from Prince Adeyanju, son of Alaafin Abiodun Adegoolu of the Old Oyo Empire. Through royal maternal lineages from Shaki and Ile-Igbon, the families of Toki and Onibudo became deeply woven into Yoruba military and cultural history.

“Itan wa ni ipilẹ ọjọ iwaju wa.”
— Our history is the foundation of our future.

Warrior Legacy

Ogun Owu

The destruction of earlier Ibadan settlements during the Owu War shaped the migration and military history of the Onibudo lineage.

Kiniun Onibudo

Ibikunle, known as “Kiniun Onibudo,” became one of the greatest Baloguns in Ibadan history through courage, loyalty, and military brilliance.

Budo Ija

The name “Onibudo” emerged from Olawolu’s exceptional ability in establishing and protecting military camps during wartime.

Historic Compounds

The settlements and compounds of Toki, Onibudo, Ibikunle, and Yeosa became part of the foundational structure of present-day Ibadan.

  • Ile Toki Onibudo — Oke Ayeye, Ibadan
  • Idi Ogun Onibudo — Idikan / Opoyeosa Junction
  • Ile Ibikunle — Historic Balogun Compound
  • Ile Yeosa — One of the largest compounds in Ibadan

Historical Timeline

1770–1789

Reign of Alaafin Abiodun Adegoolu in the Old Oyo Empire.

Early 1800s

Toki and Onibudo migrated and became established warriors in Ibadan.

1810

Re-establishment of the third Ibadan after the destruction caused by Ogun Owu.

Era of Basorun Oluyole

Onibudo rose among the most respected warriors in Ibadanland.

Legacy & Preservation

The Onibudo, Toki, and Ibikunle families remain united through shared ancestry, military heritage, and contributions to the growth of Ibadanland.
